# Evaluating Deal Q&A retrieval

Deal Q&A worked in manual testing, but "works when I try it" is not a measurement.
The tuned constants in [RetrievalOptions.cs](../src/AiService/AiService.Business/Retrieval/RetrievalOptions.cs)
— `MinScore`, `RelativeFloor`, `MaxContextChunks` — were calibrated by hand against a
handful of probe questions, and nothing proved they were right or would catch it if a
later change broke them.

This is what measuring them properly found. Two of the four headline results contradict
what I expected going in, which is the main argument for having built it.

An interactive visual companion — the parameter sweep, per-slice breakdown, score
separability, the ablation, and the generation cross-check — is at
[claude.ai/code/artifact/8f8d46c9-47a8-4b30-b66d-aea3f8783041](https://claude.ai/code/artifact/8f8d46c9-47a8-4b30-b66d-aea3f8783041).

## Method

RAG fails in two independent places, and one number hides which:

1. **Retrieval** — did the right text reach the prompt?
2. **Generation** — given that text, was the answer correct and grounded?

They are measured separately, by two scripts that share a question set but nothing else.

### The question set

100 labelled questions ([`scripts/eval-questions.json`](../scripts/eval-questions.json)),
generated by [`build_eval_set.py`](../scripts/build_eval_set.py) from the corpus itself.

The corpus PDFs are templated — every document carries a summary block of `label` /
`value` line pairs — so ground truth is *extracted*, not hand-written. Over all 521 PDFs
every label used resolves in 100% of its documents and appears exactly once. All 100
generated questions were then verified against their source PDF: label present on the
cited page, expected value present on the cited page, zero failures.

Two quality filters shaped which labels are usable:

- **Low-variance labels are excluded.** `Standard` is `ASTM E1527-21` in 43/43 Phase I
  reports; `Exclusivity period` is `30 days` in 62/62 LOIs. A question whose answer is
  identical for every property can be answered from priors, or from the *wrong*
  property's document, and would score as a retrieval success while proving nothing.
  Everything kept varies across ≥37% of the corpus.
- **Question text paraphrases the label** ("WALT" → "weighted average lease term"), so
  the eval isn't measuring literal string overlap between query and chunk.

Five slices, because they fail differently:

| Slice | n | Built from | Correct behaviour |
|---|---|---|---|
| `single-fact` | 40 | Auto-extracted label/value pairs | Retrieve the chunk holding the figure |
| `table-lookup` | 20 | Rent-roll per-tenant rows | Same, on numeric tables |
| `cross-document` | 20 | A figure two documents *disagree* about | Retrieve **both** chunks |
| `unanswerable` | 10 | Hand-written, in-domain, absent | Decline |
| `off-domain` | 10 | Hand-written nonsense | Rejected by `MinScore` |

`cross-document` uses only pairs that genuinely conflict — OM-vs-rent-roll occupancy
differs in 85/85 properties, OM-vs-LOI price in 62/62. Pairs that always agree were
excluded: either chunk alone would answer them, so they'd test nothing `single-fact`
doesn't. These are scored with strict set-recall — retrieving one of the two required
chunks is a failure, because the answer *is* the comparison.

### The two harnesses

**[`eval_retrieval.py`](../scripts/eval_retrieval.py)** — retrieval only. Stdlib-only, no
LLM, no install. Sends each question to `/ingestion/v1/search`, then re-runs
`RetrievalService`'s filter chain in Python. That duplicates ~20 lines of C#, taken
deliberately: with the filters in the harness a parameter sweep is a loop rather than a
rebuild-redeploy cycle. The ranked fetch is cached per question, so a 36-config sweep
costs 100 embedding calls rather than 3,600.

**[`eval_ragas.py`](../scripts/eval_ragas.py)** — the generation half, via the real
`/ai/v1/deals/{id}/ask` endpoint, scored with RAGAS.

## Retrieval results

Measured over **54 properties** (428 documents, 1,147 chunks):

| config | single-fact | table-lookup | cross-doc | **overall** | off-domain rejected |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| baseline (`0.15 / 0.55 / k=8`) | 0.88 | 1.00 | 0.45 | **0.80** | 0.90 |
| floors off (ablation) | 0.88 | 1.00 | 0.45 | **0.80** | 0.00 |
| **`0.20 / 0.55 / k=12`** | **0.97** | **1.00** | **0.60** | **0.89** | **1.00** |

Recall at fetch (top-20, before filtering) is 0.99 overall, so almost everything that is
lost is lost *after* the candidates are found.

> **These numbers replace an earlier run over 17 properties**, before the corpus was fully
> seeded. Every conclusion below reproduced on 3× the properties, and the recommendation's
> margin *grew* (+0.07 → +0.09) — the baseline slipped 0.82 → 0.80 on the more varied
> corpus while the recommended config held at 0.89. The first run's headline figures were
> not an artifact of a small sample, which is worth more than the figures themselves.

### 1. `MaxContextChunks` is the binding constraint, not the floors

`k=5` → 0.713, `k=8` → 0.800, `k=12` → 0.887, holding the floors fixed. Nothing else in
the sweep moves the number remotely as much. Every single `cross-document` failure had a
**full** 8-chunk context with one of the two required chunks cut — the context was full,
and the second source lost the tie.

### 2. `RelativeFloor = 0.55` is fine — I predicted it was the problem

Going in, the relative floor looked like the risky constant: at a top score of 0.50 it
kills everything below 0.275, which is exactly the mechanism that could drop the weaker
of two good hits. The sweep says otherwise. `0.00` and `0.35` are indistinguishable from
`0.55`, and `0.55` *reduces* context (10.4 → 9.6 chunks at `k=12`) at **zero** recall
cost. `0.70` is where it starts cutting (0.887 → 0.863). Keep it.

### 3. `MinScore` does nothing for recall — its only job is off-domain rejection

Recall@final is byte-identical at `0.10`, `0.15`, and `0.20`, because real hits score far
above all three. But `0.15` is marginally too low: *"What is the capital of Mongolia?"*
scored **0.166** and leaked a chunk into a prompt. `0.20` rejects all 10 off-domain
questions at no recall cost.

### 4. The ablation isolates exactly what the floors buy

Turning both floors off changes positive recall not at all (0.80 → 0.80) and collapses
off-domain rejection from 0.90 to 0.00, with 6.8 junk chunks per off-domain question
reaching the prompt. That is their entire function, cleanly measured.

### What the file already said, confirmed

`RetrievalOptions` documents that in-domain-but-absent questions score 0.29–0.32, inside
the on-topic range, so no threshold can separate them. Measured: those questions score
**0.26–0.49** and abstention is **0.00 at every threshold tested**. The floor cannot do
this job and the file is right that the system prompt has to — which the generation
results below confirm from the other side, with 10/10 declined. This is the one
prediction in the codebase that the eval confirms rather than overturns.

## Generation results (RAGAS)

RAGAS is used for the judged metrics only. The principle: **use RAGAS where the metric
needs an LLM judge and the prompt is the hard part; write your own where the metric is
arithmetic.** `recall@k` and `MRR` are ten lines each and already standard vocabulary, so
`eval_retrieval.py` computes them directly; `Faithfulness` needs a tuned claim-
decomposition prompt, which is what a library is worth paying for.

### The judge is cross-provider, deliberately

Deal Q&A generates on **`claude-sonnet-5`**, called directly through the Anthropic SDK
([AnthropicOptions.cs](../src/AiService/AiService.Business/AnthropicOptions.cs)). The
judge is therefore **OpenAI**, reached through a `judge-default` route on the existing
LiteLLM proxy. Judging a model's output with the same model — or the same family —
measures self-preference as much as it measures faithfulness. Routing through the proxy
keeps swapping judges a one-line config change.

### Scores

> Re-measured on the full 54-property corpus (428 documents), same run that produced the
> retrieval numbers above: 100 fresh answers regenerated through `claude-sonnet-5`
> (`--regenerate`), scored with `judge-default` (`openai/gpt-4o`) over 3 passes.

All 80 answerable questions, judged metrics averaged over 3 passes:

| metric | score | spread over 3 passes |
|---|---|---|
| `Faithfulness` (LLM-judged) | **0.932** | ± 0.017 |
| `ResponseRelevancy` (LLM-judged) | **0.655** | ± 0.011 |
| `NonLLMContextRecall` | 0.838 | ± 0.000 |
| `NonLLMContextPrecisionWithReference` | 0.624 | ± 0.000 |
| answer states the expected value (ours, deterministic) | **0.825** | — |

Faithfulness at 0.93 is the headline: answers are well grounded in the context they were
given. `ResponseRelevancy` at 0.65 is lower and is worth reading with care — it penalises
answers that add hedging and source attribution, which this system's prompt explicitly
asks for, so it is measuring house style as much as relevance.

Value correctness by slice mostly tracks retrieval, with one slice that doesn't:

| slice | states the expected value | recall@final (baseline config) |
|---|---|---|
| `single-fact` | 0.85 | 0.88 |
| `table-lookup` | 1.00 | 1.00 |
| `cross-document` | **0.60** | **0.45** |

`single-fact` and `table-lookup` fit the expected shape — generation is not the
bottleneck where the chunk reaches the prompt. `cross-document` breaks the pattern this
run: the model states the correct composite value in 60% of cases despite retrieval
delivering both required sources only 45% of the time, the reverse of the earlier
17-property result (0.45 states / 0.40 recall, generation trailing retrieval as
expected). Not diagnosed further here — worth a follow-up look at whether the model is
answering correctly from a single source often enough to matter, or whether
`answer_contains_expected()`'s digit-match is more permissive on this slice than
`gold_hit()`'s strict set-recall.

### Abstention: the prompt does the job the floor cannot

This is the cleanest confirmation in the whole exercise. `RetrievalOptions` argues that
no score threshold can separate "in-domain but absent" from "answerable", and that the
system prompt has to carry it. Both halves measured:

| | retrieval-level abstention | prompt-level decline |
|---|---|---|
| `unanswerable` (10) | **0.00** — nothing filtered | **10/10 declined** |
| `off-domain` (10) | 1.00 at `MinScore 0.20` | **10/10 declined** |

Against 1 false decline in 80 answerable questions. The floor catches none of the
in-domain-absent questions, exactly as documented, and the prompt catches all of them.

### The cross-check that validated the whole thing

RAGAS's non-LLM context metrics are free and deterministic, which makes them a test of
the harness rather than of the system. On the original 17-property build, the 20-question
subset where every question has a single gold reference gave exact agreement between the
two implementations — `eval_retrieval.py` recall@final and RAGAS `NonLLMContextRecall`
both 0.950. That check is about harness correctness, not corpus size, so it isn't
re-run here; it stood once and there's no reason byte-identical code would disagree now.

Across all 80 answerable questions on the current 54-property corpus, the two still
diverge in the same direction — `eval_retrieval.py` recall@final (baseline config, all
positive slices, same 80-question scope RAGAS scores) is **0.80**, RAGAS
`NonLLMContextRecall` is **0.838**, a 3.8-point gap (down from 5.6 points on the smaller
corpus). The mechanism is the same as before: `cross-document` questions carry two gold
references; `eval_retrieval.py` scores them with strict set-recall (one of two is a
failure, because the answer *is* the comparison), while RAGAS averages each reference
independently and awards 0.5 for retrieving one. Strict set-recall is the right measure
for this feature, which is why the deterministic harness is the one quoted above.

Getting to that agreement took three corrections, and each was a real bug rather than a
metric quirk:

1. **Citations are not the context.** I first passed RAGAS the answer's *citations* —
   only the sources the model chose to reference, a subset. Every score was 0.000. For
   *"how much is the seller asking?"* the offering memorandum was in the context but
   uncited, so the gold reference matched nothing. Fixed by reconstructing the true
   context with the same deterministic search+filter chain.
2. **Edit distance is bounded by length ratio.** The non-LLM metrics score by Levenshtein
   similarity, so a 300-character reference against an 870-character chunk cannot exceed
   ~0.34 — under threshold even for a perfect match. Sizing the reference to a citation
   snippet instead of to a chunk kept the scores at zero for a second reason.
3. **A reconstructed passage is not the chunk.** A window cut from page text never aligns
   with where the splitter actually split; it scored 0.300 against a retrieval that was
   genuinely 0.95. Fixed by using the real indexed chunk as the reference — fetched by
   pinning search to the gold `documentId` and accepting only a chunk containing the gold
   anchor, so the ranker is being used to read the index, not to judge itself.

The lesson generalises: a judged metric that disagrees with a deterministic one is
telling you something, and all three times it was the harness that was wrong. Had this
started with RAGAS alone, all three bugs would have shipped as findings about the system.

### `FactualCorrectness` was dropped, on purpose

It decomposes `reference` into claims and scores overlap — but our ground truth is a bare
value (`$67,834,000`), not a reference answer. There is nothing to decompose, and it
scored a flat 0.000 for that reason rather than because the answers were wrong. Value
correctness here is exact-match arithmetic, so the harness computes it directly (digit
comparison, so `$67,834,000` matches `67,834,000`). Same principle that keeps `recall@k`
out of RAGAS.

### Judge variance is not optional rigour here

Judge scores cannot be pinned on this stack. `claude-sonnet-5` **rejects** `temperature`
outright — the API removed it, and ai-service already documents this
([AnthropicOptions.cs](../src/AiService/AiService.Business/AnthropicOptions.cs)). The
proxy's `drop_params: true` silently drops it rather than erroring. So every judged
number is reported as a mean across repeated passes with its spread, because a single
figure would imply a precision that does not exist.

The measured spread also validates the setup: the two non-LLM metrics moved **± 0.000**
across three passes while the judged ones moved ± 0.021 and ± 0.011. The variance is
isolated to the judge, which is what it should be — anything else would mean the pipeline
itself was non-deterministic. Faithfulness varying by ~2 points run to run is the
practical resolution limit: differences smaller than that are noise, not signal.

## Limitations

These bound what the numbers mean, and matter more than any single figure above.

- **The haystack is small, and that is the load-bearing caveat.** Retrieval is scoped to
  one deal, and a deal holds roughly 17 chunks (1,147 across 68 deals), so `FetchTopK = 20`
  fetches essentially all of it. `recall@fetch` of 0.99 is therefore close to free, and
  every number here describes *this* corpus rather than retrieval in general. Widening the
  corpus 3× did not change this — it is a property of deal-scoped retrieval, not of the
  sample size. It also means the hybrid-BM25 idea I started with was solving a problem
  this system does not have: `table-lookup` scores 1.00 on dense retrieval alone.
- **Coverage is 54 of 83 corpus properties.** Up from 17 — the cap was never the documents
  but the *deals*, since seeding matches a document set to a deal by `propertyName`. 51
  missing deals were created ([create_deals_for_corpus.py](../scripts/create_deals_for_corpus.py)),
  taking the corpus from 118 documents to 428. The remaining gap is 14 corpus properties
  with no listing to create a deal from, plus one genuinely ambiguous name; the eval set
  samples 54 of the 68 seeded deals at 1–3 questions each.
- **Templated documents flatter extraction.** Real PDFs are messier, and 100% label
  resolution would not survive contact with them.
- **Small slices are noisy.** `cross-document` and `table-lookup` have 20 questions each,
  so one failure moves a slice by 5 points. Read the counts, not just the percentages,
  and don't over-read small differences between sweep configurations.
- **Auto-generated questions inherit the generator's phrasing.** Paraphrasing the labels
  mitigates this but does not eliminate it.

## Reproducing

Requires the stack up (`docker compose up -d`) with documents seeded.

```bash
python3 scripts/rebuild_manifest.py && python3 scripts/build_eval_set.py
```

```bash
python3 scripts/eval_retrieval.py --sweep
```

```bash
scripts/.venv/bin/python scripts/eval_ragas.py
```

`eval_retrieval.py` is stdlib-only and needs no install. `eval_ragas.py` needs its own
venv (`scripts/requirements-eval.txt`).

**The LLM-judged tier is opt-in.** A bare `eval_ragas.py` run scores only the free,
deterministic metrics — the half that cross-checks the deterministic harness. Add
`--llm-judge` for Faithfulness, ResponseRelevancy, and FactualCorrectness; it prints its
expected call volume before spending anything:

```bash
scripts/.venv/bin/python scripts/eval_ragas.py --llm-judge --repeats 3
```

That is the command that produced the judged numbers above, and it is the expensive one:
80 samples × 3 metrics × 3 passes is roughly 1,300 judge calls. `--repeats` only matters
with `--llm-judge` — the non-LLM metrics measured 0.000 spread across three passes, so
repeating them returns identical numbers. Generated answers are cached in
`eval-answers.json`, so re-scoring never re-pays for generation.

## Recommended change

`MinScore 0.15 → 0.20`, `MaxContextChunks 8 → 12`, `RelativeFloor` unchanged at `0.55`.

Overall recall 0.80 → 0.89, `single-fact` 0.88 → 0.97, `cross-document` 0.45 → 0.60,
off-domain rejection 0.90 → 1.00. The cost is ~34% more context (7.2 → 9.6 chunks,
6,145 → 8,272 characters per prompt).

`cross-document` at 0.60 is the remaining weak spot and is not a tuning problem: some of
those failures don't retrieve both chunks even at top-20, which is a ranking limitation
rather than a filtering one.
